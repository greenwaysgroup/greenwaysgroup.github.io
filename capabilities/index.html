01 / Capabilities

Specialist interior systems, clearly defined.

GWG works across three confirmed service categories. The exact materials, performance requirements, interfaces and responsibilities are agreed against the project specification and package information.

A useful enquiry starts with the relevant drawings, specification, location and programme. These establish the required system, the surrounding interfaces and the boundaries of the package.

01

Suspended Ceilings

Suspended ceiling installation for defined commercial interior packages, coordinated with the surrounding structure, partitions and building services.

A suspended ceiling forms a secondary ceiling below the structural soffit. Depending on the specified system, it can create a finished ceiling plane, organise lighting and other services, provide access to the void above, or contribute to acoustic and fire-performance requirements. GWG installs suspended ceilings where they form part of an agreed commercial package. The system type, materials, levels, performance criteria and interface responsibilities remain governed by the project drawings, specification and approved details.

What the service does

  • Creates the specified ceiling plane using an exposed grid, concealed MF construction or another documented system.
  • Coordinates openings, perimeters, access points, lighting and other service interfaces shown in the project information.
  • Delivers the visible line, level and finish required for the ceiling package.
  • Keeps system performance tied to the specified build-up and supporting evidence rather than generic claims.

Typical commercial context

Suspended ceilings are used across commercial interiors where the design needs a controlled finished surface beneath the structure. The selected build-up may also need to accommodate a service void, access requirements, acoustic treatment, feature geometry or defined fire performance.

Delivery approach

The package should establish setting-out information, ceiling levels, system build-up, perimeters, penetrations, access requirements and interfaces with MEP and adjoining partitions. GWG uses that information to plan and install the defined works; changes to the system or performance requirement need to be resolved through the project team and documented before they are built.

02

Structural Framing Systems

Light-gauge steel framing installed to the project design, forming defined wall zones and interfaces within the wider building assembly.

Structural framing systems, commonly referred to as SFS, use light-gauge steel sections to form a precise framed construction. Infill framing can sit between the floors and soffits of the primary structure, while other arrangements may be positioned around the structural frame. GWG installs SFS where it is included in the agreed package and where suitable design information is issued. Structural design, engineering, system selection, supply, warranty and façade responsibility remain exactly as allocated by the contract and project team.

What the service does

  • Forms the specified light-gauge steel frame at internal or external wall zones shown in the project design.
  • Establishes accurate lines, openings and connection points for the documented wall build-up.
  • Coordinates with the primary structure and with adjoining board, insulation and façade interfaces.
  • Provides a buildable installation only when the design responsibility and system information are clear.

Typical commercial context

SFS is commonly used where a lightweight, accurately set-out frame is required around the main structure, including external wall infill and other defined framing applications. Its relationship with deflection, floor edges, openings, sheathing, insulation and façade systems makes interface information particularly important.

Delivery approach

Before installation, the package needs coordinated drawings, structural and system details, setting-out information, connection requirements and clear responsibility for the complete wall build-up. GWG works to the issued design and agreed scope. Ambiguous or changed interfaces should be resolved before they are concealed or passed to the following trade.

03

Partitions and Plastering

Partition, plasterboard and plastering work that turns a coordinated internal layout into defined rooms, linings and finished surfaces.

Partitions divide internal space and provide the framework for plasterboard linings and finishes. A commercial drylining package may combine metal studs, tracks, insulation, boards, wall linings and details around doors, glazing, services and adjoining construction. The specified build-up determines requirements such as fire resistance, acoustic separation, moisture exposure and robustness. GWG installs partitions and plastering work within the agreed package; the required board types, layers, finish route and performance evidence come from the project specification and approved system details.

What the service does

  • Forms internal partitions and wall linings to the documented layout and setting-out information.
  • Coordinates studs, boards, openings, service zones and junctions with the surrounding construction.
  • Prepares surfaces for the specified plaster, skim, tape-and-joint or other documented finish.
  • Keeps fire, acoustic, moisture and duty requirements connected to the complete tested or specified system.

Typical commercial context

Partitions and plasterboard linings are used throughout commercial fit-out and refurbishment work to divide spaces, line backgrounds and create finished internal surfaces. The package may include straight or curved construction, bulkheads and coordinated openings, but only where those items are shown in the agreed scope.

Delivery approach

Useful information includes partition types, heights, board build-ups, duty and performance requirements, door and glazing details, service penetrations, head conditions and finish schedules. GWG installs against that coordinated information and the agreed sequence, with interfaces reviewed before boarding and finishing make them difficult to inspect.
